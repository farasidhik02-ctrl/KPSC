## 1. VAIKUNTA SWAMIKAL

### Q1. Who was Vaikunta Swamikal, and what are the important facts about his early life and identity?

**Answer:**

- Original name: **Muthukutty**
- Born: **1809**
- Birthplace: **Swamithope, Kanyakumari district**, then part of Travancore
- Died: **1851**
- Also known as **Ayya Vaikundar, Siva Narayana and Vaikunda Swami**
- Founder of the **Ayyavazhi faith**
- Ayyavazhis believe him to be the **first and foremost Purna avatar of Vishnu (Narayana)**.
- He emerged as an important early opponent of caste discrimination and social inequality.

### Q2. Why was Vaikunta Swamikal arrested by the Travancore government?

**Answer:**

Vaikunta Swamikal made several controversial statements against the existing rulers.

- He referred to the **Travancore king as the “Devil in Ananthapuri.”**
- He described British rule as the **“Rule of White Devils.”**
- His popularity increased and large numbers of people began gathering around him.
- A complaint was lodged against him with **Swathi Thirunal Rama Varma, King of Travancore**.
- The king arrested Vaikundar in **1838**.
- He was imprisoned in **Singarathoppe jail**.
- He remained imprisoned for **110 days**.
- He was released on **26 March 1839**.
- Swathi Thirunal released him on the advice of **Thycaud Ayya**.
- Thycaud Ayya was the **Guru of Swathi Thirunal Maharaj** and was also a **disciple of Ayya Vaikundar**.

### Q3. What were the major contributions of Vaikunta Swamikal?

**Answer:**

- Founded the **Ayya Vazhi movement**, a monotheistic and anti-caste spiritual tradition.
- Challenged caste discrimination, particularly discrimination against the **Nadar community**.
- Established the **Samathwa Samajam**, one of the earliest social reform movements in South India.
- Advocated:
  - Social equality
  - Abolition of priestly dominance
  - Dignity for oppressed people

### Q4. What is the important work associated with Vaikunta Swamikal, and what is his present relevance?

**Answer:**

**Important work:**

- **Akilathirattu Ammanai**
- It is a scripture composed by his followers.
- It describes his teachings and philosophy.

**Current relevance:**

- Regarded as a pioneer of anti-caste movements in **Kerala and Tamil Nadu**.
- His teachings continue to inspire **Dalit and backward-caste movements**.
- **Ayya Vazhi** continues to be followed by thousands of people.
- He is recognized as a precursor to later reformers such as **Sree Narayana Guru**.

# 2. THYCAUD AYYA (AYYA SWAMIKAL)

### Q5. Who was Thycaud Ayya?

**Answer:**

- Original name: **Subbarayan**
- Born: **1814**
- Birthplace: **Thycaud, Thiruvananthapuram**
- Died: **1909**
- Also known as **Thycaud Ayyavu Swamikal / Ayyaguru Swamikal**
- He was a **spiritualist and social reformer**.
- He was the first to challenge caste customs in Kerala during a period when caste restrictions and untouchability were at their peak.

### Q6. What predictions are attributed to Thycaud Ayya?

**Answer:**

Thycaud Ayya was an accomplished astrologer and was believed to possess the **siddhi to predict future events**.

His predictions included:

- The younger Maharani’s son would become the **last Maharaja**.
- **North India would eventually separate**.
- **Ayyankali** would be appointed to a participative administrative body to help the downtrodden.
- He predicted the **date of his own Samadhi**.

According to the source, these predictions came true:

- Ayyankali was nominated to the **Sri Moolam Assembly** and honoured by the rulers.
- Ayyankali’s statue was later unveiled by **Prime Minister Indira Gandhi**.
- **[Maharaja Chithira Tirunal** became the last King of Travancore.](https://www.keralapscnotes.qd.je/content/history-of-travancore-from-marthanda-varma-to-sree-chithirathirunnal/)
- Thycaud Ayya attained Samadhi on the date he had forecast.
- India was divided into **India and Pakistan** about fifty years after his prediction.

### Q7. What were the major contributions and teachings of Thycaud Ayya?

**Answer:**

- Spiritual leader and social reformer.
- Guru of **Chattampi Swamikal** and **Sree Narayana Guru**.
- Promoted:
  - Universal brotherhood
  - Peace
  - Religious harmony
- Believed in **Advaita Vedanta or non-dualism**.
- Advocated spiritual equality among all people.
- Encouraged:
  - Education
  - Meditation
  - Simplicity in life

He left **no written works directly**, but his oral teachings deeply influenced Kerala's reform tradition.

### Q8. What is the present significance of Thycaud Ayya?

**Answer:**

- Remembered as a **bridge between tradition and reform**.
- Mentored important Kerala Renaissance leaders.
- Represents a **non-violent and spiritual approach to social change**.
- Statues and memorials have been established in his honour, particularly in **Thiruvananthapuram**.

# 3. ARATTUPUZHA VELAYUDHA PANICKER

### Q9. Who was Arattupuzha Velayudha Panicker?

**Answer:**

- Born: **1825**
- Birthplace: **Arattupuzha, Kayamkulam**
- Died: **1874**
- Also known as **Kallisseril Velayudha Perumal**
- He was a 19th-century **social reformer and warrior**.
- He fought against several social evils prevalent in Kerala.
- He was a wealthy **Ezhava landlord** who used his resources to uplift marginalized communities.

### Q10. What was the importance of the temple constructed by Velayudha Panicker?

**Answer:**

- He built the **Mangalam Shiva Temple**, also known as the **Edakkad Jnaneswara Temple**, at Arattupuzha.
- It was built in **1852**.
- The temple was opened to people of **all castes and religions**.
- He challenged caste restrictions concerning temple entry.
- He also challenged discriminatory dress codes.
- He constructed temples such as the **Mangalam Temple at Kayamkulam**, where lower castes could worship freely.

### Q11. What major social struggles were led by Arattupuzha Velayudha Panicker?

**Answer:**

He organized several important struggles for the rights and dignity of marginalized communities:

**Achipudava Samaram – 1859**

- Conducted at **Kayamkulam**.
- A struggle for the right of women of backward communities to wear a **lengthy lower garment extending below the knees**.

**Karshaka Thozhilali Samaram**

- Organized by Velayudha Panicker.
- Described in the source as the **first-ever strike by agricultural labourers in Kerala**.
- The strike succeeded.

**Ethappu Samaram – 1859**

- A continuation of the Achipudava Samaram.
- Fought for the right of women of backward communities to **wear upper cloth**.

**Mukkuthi Samaram – 1860**

- Led at **Pandalam**.
- Fought for the right of women belonging to marginalized communities to wear **nose studs and other gold ornaments**.

These successful struggles played an important role in increasing the dignity of marginalized women.

### Q12. What was Velayudha Panicker's contribution to Kathakali?

**Answer:**

- In **1861**, he established the **first Kathakali Yogam for Ezhavas**.
- Kathakali performed by Ezhavas and members of other backward communities was staged for the first time.

### Q13. What was the Salagramam incident associated with Velayudha Panicker?

**Answer:**

- In **1869**, Brahmin priest **Tharananellur Nambudiri** was travelling to the **Padmanabhaswamy Temple** for **Murajapam**.
- Murajapam involved the chanting of prayers and recitation of **three Vedas for 56 days**.
- His **Salagramam**, a sacred stone, was robbed in the backwaters of Kayamkulam.
- **Kayamkulam Kochunni** had stolen it.
- Palace and police officials failed to find him.
- Velayudha Panicker was then entrusted with the task.
- He captured Kayamkulam Kochunni and submitted him to the **Diwan**.
- The Salagramam was recovered and returned to Tharananellur Nambudiri.
- Panicker was honoured by the King.
- **Velayudha Chekavar** was given the title **“Panicker”** by the King of [Travancore](https://www.keralapscnotes.qd.je/content/history-of-travancore-from-marthanda-varma-to-sree-chithirathirunnal/).

### Q14. How did Arattupuzha Velayudha Panicker die, and why is his death significant?

**Answer:**

- In **1874**, some upper-caste men plotted against him.
- He was murdered while sleeping during a **boat journey at night**.
- They obtained the help of a converted Ezhava Muslim named **Thoppiyitta Kittan**.
- Velayudha Panicker was **48 years old** at the time of his death.
- He is described as the **first martyr of the Kerala Renaissance**.
- He had defied Nair and Namboothiri dominance and organized armed resistance when necessary.
- He was assassinated by upper-caste opponents who feared his increasing influence.

**Current relevance:**

- Recognized as one of the earliest martyrs of Kerala's social reform movement.
- His legacy is celebrated through books, plays and public commemorations.
- Seen as a symbol of courage and resistance to caste oppression.

# 4. CHATTAMPI SWAMIKAL

### Q15. Who was Chattampi Swamikal?

**Answer:**

- Original name: **Ayyappan**
- Also known as **Kunjan Pillai**
- Born: **25 August 1853**
- Birthplace: **Kollur, Thiruvananthapuram**
- Died: **5 May 1924**
- Ayyappan Pillai, better known as Chattampi Swamikal, was a **Hindu sage and social reformer**.
- His ideas influenced many social, religious, literary and political organizations and movements in Kerala.
- His work gave a voice to marginalized people.

### Q16. What were the major ideas and contributions of Chattampi Swamikal?

**Answer:**

- Philosopher, yogi and social reformer.
- Challenged:
  - Caste hierarchy
  - Brahminical dominance
- Advocated:
  - Spiritual freedom
  - Social equality
  - Upliftment of women
  - Upliftment of marginalized communities
- Rejected the idea that only Brahmins had access to **Vedic knowledge**.
- Promoted inter-religious harmony among:
  - Hindus
  - Muslims
  - Christians

### Q17. What are the important works of Chattampi Swamikal?

**Answer:**

- **Pracheena Malayalam** – debunked caste myths and argued that all people had equal access to knowledge.
- **Vedadhikara Nirupanam** – argued for the right of non-Brahmins to study the Vedas.
- **Christumatha Saram** – expressed respect for all religions and emphasized unity.

**Current relevance:**

- Regarded as a forerunner of **rationalism and social criticism in Kerala**.
- His teachings continue to influence social thinkers, reformers and spiritual seekers.

# 5. SREE NARAYANA GURU

### Q18. Who was Sree Narayana Guru, and what does the source state about his birth and family?

**Answer:**

The introductory details give:

- Born: **20 August 1855**
- Birthplace: **Chempazhanthy, near Thiruvananthapuram**
- Died: **20 September 1928**

The later biographical section gives his birth as **20 August 1856**.

- Also known as **Narayanan / Nanu**
- Father: **Madan Asan**
- Mother: **Kuttiyamma**
- Born into an **Ezhava family of Ayurvedic physicians**.
- Birthplace: Chempazhanthy near Thiruvananthapuram in the erstwhile state of Travancore.

He became a philosopher, spiritual leader and social reformer who led a movement against injustice in Kerala's caste-ridden society.

### Q19. What was Narayana Guru's famous philosophy, and what was Daiva Dasakam?

**Answer:**

His famous statement was:

**“One Caste, One Religion, One God for all human beings.”**

The source also gives the form:

**“One Caste, One Religion, One God for Man.”**

His goals included:

- Spiritual enlightenment
- Social equality
- Equality across caste divisions

He wrote the Advaita poem **Daiva Dasakam**, one of the most widely used poems in Kerala for community prayer.

### Q20. What education and spiritual training did Narayana Guru receive?

**Answer:**

- Unlike other Ezhavas who generally confined Sanskrit reading to Ayurvedic works, Narayana Guru studied **religious texts**.
- His early education followed the **Gurukula system** under **Chempazhanthi Mootha Pillai**.
- His mother died when he was **15**.
- At **21**, he went to Central Travancore.
- He studied under Sanskrit scholar **Raman Pillai Asan**.
- Raman Pillai Asan taught him:
  - Vedas
  - Upanishads
  - Sanskrit literature
  - Logical rhetoric

### Q21. What happened during Narayana Guru's early adult life before he became a major reformer?

**Answer:**

- A year after going to study under Raman Pillai Asan, he married **Kaliamma**.
- He soon disassociated himself from the marriage to begin his public life as a social reformer.
- He returned to his village in **1881** when his father became seriously ill.
- He started a village school and taught local children.
- This earned him the name **Nanu Asan**.
- He later travelled through Kerala and Tamil Nadu.
- During these travels, he met **Chattampi Swamikal**.
- Chattampi Swamikal introduced him to **Ayyavu Swamikal**.
- From Ayyavu Swamikal, Guru learned **meditation and yoga**.
- He eventually reached the **Pillathadam cave at Maruthwamala**.
- He established a hermitage there.
- He practised meditation there for the next **eight years**.

### Q22. What was the Aruvippuram Pratishta, and why was it significant?

**Answer:**

- In **1888**, Narayana Guru visited **Aruvippuram**.
- He meditated in a cave near the **Neyyar River**.
- His first and one of his prominent disciples, **Sivalingadasa Swamikal**, discovered him there.
- Sivalingadasa Swamikal belonged to an **orthodox Nair family**.
- Guru took a rock from the deepest part of the Neyyar River.
- The place was a whirlpool sinkhole called **Sankaran Kuzhi**.
- It is believed that sage **Agastya** had given his worshipping Shiva Linga to the Neyyar River at Sankaran Kuzhi before leaving.
- Guru consecrated the rock as an idol of **Shiva**.
- The site became known as the **Aruvippuram Shiva Temple**.

The consecration became known as the **Aruvippuram Pratishta**.

Upper-caste Brahmins questioned Guru's right to consecrate the idol.

His famous reply was:

**“This is not a Brahmin Shiva but an Ezhava Shiva.”**

The statement later became a famous anti-caste quotation.

### Q23. How was the SNDP Yogam established?

**Answer:**

- The **Sree Narayana Dharma Paripalana Yogam (SNDP Yogam)** was founded at Aruvippuram.
- Date: **15 May 1903**
- It was established through the efforts of **Padmanabhan Palpu**.
- **Narayana Guru** became its founder president.
- It organized social reform activities.

### Q24. What educational and temple-building activities did Narayana Guru undertake?

**Answer:**

- In **1904**, Guru shifted his base to **Sivagiri near Varkala**.
- He opened a school for children belonging to the lower sections of society.
- The school provided **free education irrespective of caste**.
- It took seven years to construct a temple there.
- **Sarada Mutt** was built in **1912**.

He also constructed temples in:

- Thrissur
- Kannur
- Anchuthengu
- Thalassery
- Kozhikode
- Mangalore

He travelled widely, including to **Sri Lanka, then called Ceylon**.

- His final visit to Ceylon was in **1926**.

### Q25. What were the circumstances surrounding Narayana Guru's final days?

**Answer:**

- His last public function was a meeting at **Pallathuruthy**.
- He became ill soon afterwards.
- He received treatment at:
  - Aluva
  - Thrissur
  - Palakkad
  - Chennai

Ayurvedic physicians who treated him included:

- **Cholayil Mami Vaidyar**
- **Panappally Krishnan Vaidyar**
- **Thycauttu Divakaran Moos**

Allopathic physicians included:

- **Krishnan Thampi**
- **Panikker**
- **Palpu**
- A European physician named **Noble**

He returned to **Sarada Mutt** and died on **20 September 1928**, aged **72**.

### Q26. What are the important works and continuing significance of Sree Narayana Guru?

**Answer:**

Important works include:

- **Atmopadesa Śatakam** – 100 verses on self-realization
- **Anukamba Dasakam**
- **Jathi Nirnayam**
- **Jathi Lakshanam**
- **Gajendra Moksham Vanchipattu**
- **Daivadasakam**

He wrote numerous poems and philosophical works promoting:

- Equality
- Self-respect
- Spiritual unity

**Current relevance:**

- Continues to inspire social justice movements.
- Influences secular thought and educational initiatives.
- His birthday is celebrated as **Sree Narayana Guru Jayanti**, a public holiday in Kerala.

# 6. POYKAYIL APPACHAN (POYKAYIL YOHANNAN)

### Q27. Who was Poykayil Appachan?

**Answer:**

- Also known as **Poykayil Yohannan**
- Also known as **Poykayil Kumara Gurudevan**
- Born: **1879**
- Birthplace: **Eraviperoor, Pathanamthitta district**
- Died: **1939**
- Spiritual leader
- Poet
- Dalit emancipator
- Renaissance leader
- Founder of the **Prathyaksha Raksha Daiva Sabha (PRDS)**, meaning **“God's Society of Obvious Salvation.”**

PRDS was a socio-religious movement dedicated to the spiritual liberation and social upliftment of Kerala's oppressed castes.

### Q28. Why did Poykayil Yohannan leave Christianity and establish the PRDS?

**Answer:**

- Yohannan joined the **Marthoma Church**, a reformist sect among Syrian Christians.
- He realized that Dalits were treated as an inferior class and left it.
- He then joined the **Brethren Mission**.
- He experienced similar caste discrimination there.
- He concluded that Indian Christian communities continued to practise caste discrimination.
- He believed this violated the fundamental principles of Christianity.

In **1909**:

- Yohannan left Christianity.
- He established his own religious protest movement, the **Prathyaksha Raksha Daiva Sabha**.
- Afterwards, he became known as **Poikayil Appachan or Kumara Gurudevan**.
- He advocated spiritual liberation.
- He sought to empower and consolidate Dalits.
- He promoted a creed in which Dalit castes would be free from discrimination.

### Q29. What were Poykayil Appachan's major contributions and literary significance?

**Answer:**

- Protested against caste oppression within both **Christianity and Hinduism**.
- Founded **PRDS in 1909** to liberate Dalits from caste-based slavery.
- Spoke for the cultural and historical rights of Dalits.
- Argued that Dalit stories had been erased from official religion and history.
- His oral poetry and hymns described:
  - Pain of untouchability
  - Search for identity
  - Search for dignity
- His works were preserved in the **PRDS tradition**, though he did not formally compile them.

**Current relevance:**

- Considered a pioneer of **Dalit theology and literature in Kerala**.
- Increasingly recognized in academic and literary circles as a voice of **subaltern history**.

# 7. AYYANKALI

### Q30. Who was Ayyankali, and what social conditions influenced his work?

**Answer:**

- Born: **28 August 1863**
- Birthplace: **Venganoor, Thiruvananthapuram**
- Died: **18 June 1941**
- Known as **Mahatma Ayyankali**
- Social reformer
- Educator
- Economist
- Lawmaker
- Revolutionary leader
- Known as the **“King of Pulaya.”**

He worked for oppressed people in the princely state of **Travancore**.

During his lifetime, the region was deeply divided by caste and was described as a **“mad house” of castes**.

- Pulayars were regarded as slaves of the agrarian society.
- They faced severe discrimination, particularly from landowning castes including the **Nair caste**.

### Q31. How did Ayyankali initially respond to caste oppression?

**Answer:**

- Social injustice led him to join his Pulayar friends after their workday.
- They sang and danced to folk music protesting their conditions.
- Some joined him in forming a group that challenged members of oppressive castes.
- These confrontations sometimes resulted in physical fights.
- His popularity earned him the names:
  - **Urpillai**
  - **Moothapullai**
- These roughly mean **“Leader of the Land” or “Elder Leader.”**

Personal life:

- Married **Chellamma** in **1888**.
- They had **seven children**.

### Q32. What were Ayyankali's major struggles for Dalit rights?

**Answer:**

- Fought against:
  - Caste oppression
  - Untouchability
  - Denial of education to lower castes

**Villuvandi Samaram – 1893**

- Ayyankali rode a **bullock cart or Villuvandi** on a public road.
- This openly defied caste laws restricting Dalits from using public roads.

**Education – 1904**

- Started the **first school for Dalit children in Kerala**.
- This resulted in backlash from upper castes.

**Sadhujana Paripalana Sangham – 1907**

- Established to organize and lead Dalits in their struggle for rights.

### Q33. What was Ayyankali's role in politics and labour reform?

**Answer:**

- Became the **first Dalit nominated to the Travancore Legislative Council in 1910**.
- Advocated:
  - Labour rights
  - Temple entry
  - Land for the poor

**Current relevance:**

- Symbol of resistance and pioneer of Dalit empowerment in Kerala.
- Remembered annually through tributes.
- His work helped pave the way for the **Temple Entry Proclamation of 1936**.

# 8. PANDIT KARUPPAN

### Q34. Who was Pandit Karuppan?

**Answer:**

- Full name: **Kandathil Karuppan**
- Born: **24 May 1885**
- Birthplace: **Cheranalloor, Ernakulam**
- Died: **23 March 1938**
- Community: **Dheevara**, a fisherfolk caste
- Sanskrit scholar
- Poet
- Dramatist
- Social reformer

He emerged from Ernakulam in **Cochin State** and became a major crusader against untouchability and social evils.

He was called the **“Lincoln of Kerala”** for helping socio-economically and educationally backward communities advance.

### Q35. What social issues did Pandit Karuppan campaign against?

**Answer:**

He belonged to a community of inland fishermen engaged in localized fishing in backwaters and rivers.

As the first human rights activist of Cochin State, he used his literary and organizational abilities to combat:

- Illiteracy
- Social injustice
- Casteism
- Superstitions

He also campaigned for the empowerment of the **Dheevara community**.

### Q36. What were Pandit Karuppan's major political, educational and literary contributions?

**Answer:**

- Worked for upliftment of backward communities, especially fishing communities.
- Fought for:
  - Education
  - Dignity
  - Representation of backward castes
- Became a member of the **Cochin Legislative Council**.
- Became the **first Dheevara caste representative** in the Council.
- Advocated establishment of schools and libraries for marginalized communities.

Important works:

- **Baalakalesam**
- **Udyanavirunnu**
- **Jathikummi**

His poems criticized caste discrimination and demanded social change.

His writing combined **literary excellence with reformist ideals**.

**Current relevance:**

- Honoured as the **“Lincoln of Kerala”** for his oratory and reforms.
- Continues to inspire educational and social justice movements, particularly in coastal communities.

# 9. SAHODARAN AYYAPPAN

### Q37. Who was Sahodaran Ayyappan?

**Answer:**

- Full name: **Kumbalathuparambu Ayyappan**
- Born: **21 August 1889**
- Birthplace: **Cherai, Ernakulam**
- Died: **6 March 1968**
- Community: **Ezhava**
- Social reformer
- Thinker
- Rationalist
- Journalist
- Politician
- Vocal follower of **Sree Narayana Guru**

He founded:

- **Sahodara Sangam/Sahodara Sangham**
- Journal **Sahodaran**

He was also the founder editor of **Yukthivadi**.

### Q38. What was Misra Bhojanam, and how did it lead to Ayyappan being called Sahodaran Ayyappan?

**Answer:**

After graduation, Ayyappan returned from Thiruvananthapuram to **Cherai in 1917**.

He founded the **Sahodara Sangham or Brotherhood Association** to fight the caste system.

Under its leadership:

- He organized **Misra Bhojanam**, an inter-caste feast.
- Date: **30 May 1917**
- Venue: House of his nephew **Raman Pillai**
- People of all castes sat and ate under one roof.
- Around **200 people** participated.
- Participants included members of the **Pulaya caste**, who were regarded as untouchable.
- The conservative Ezhava community opposed the event.

His opponents called him **“Pulaya Ayyappan”** as a derogatory name.

- Ayyappan accepted the name as a **decoration**.

The establishment of the Sahodara Sangham also gave him the name **“Sahodaran Ayyappan,”** by which he became widely known.

### Q39. What were Sahodaran Ayyappan's major contributions, publications and political roles?

**Answer:**

- Promoted:
  - Inter-caste equality
  - Scientific temper
  - Secularism
- Was a disciple of Sree Narayana Guru.
- Later expanded Guru's ideas through a stronger **rationalist approach**.
- Founded **Sahodara Sangham in 1917**.
- Organized **Misra Bhojanam**.

Edited journals including:

- **Sahodaran**
- **Yukthivadi**
- **Velakkaran**

These publications promoted:

- Atheism
- Science
- Anti-caste ideology

Political roles:

- Served as a minister in the **Cochin Legislative Council**.
- Later served in the **Travancore-Cochin Assembly**.

### Q40. What famous slogan was coined by Sahodaran Ayyappan?

**Answer:**

He coined:

**“Jati Venda, Matam Venda, Deivam Venda, Manushyanu Manushyan Mathram.”**

Meaning:

**“No Caste, No Religion, No God, Man is Equal to Man.”**

**Current relevance:**

- Regarded as the **father of the rationalist movement in Kerala**.
- His ideas contributed to the development of:
  - Progressive thought
  - Secularism
  - Leftist politics in Kerala

# 10. K. KELAPPAN

### Q41. Who was K. Kelappan?

**Answer:**

- Full name: **Koyapalli Kelappan**
- Born: **24 August 1889**
- Birthplace: **Muchukunnu, Calicut**
- Died: **7 October 1971**
- Politician
- Independence activist
- Educationist
- Journalist
- Leading figure of the **Indian National Congress in Kerala**
- Popularly known as **“Kerala Gandhi.”**

After independence, he held positions in various Gandhian organizations.

He was:

- Co-founder and first president of the **Nair Service Society**
- Founder of the **Kerala Kshetra Samrakshana Samiti or Temple Protection Movement**

### Q42. What were K. Kelappan's major contributions to social reform and the freedom struggle?

**Answer:**

- Known as Kerala Gandhi for his commitment to **non-violence and social reform**.
- Played an important role in the **Vaikom Satyagraha of 1924**, which opposed caste-based restrictions on roads near temples.
- Co-founded the **Nair Service Society (NSS)**.
- Later concentrated on inter-caste unity and social reform.
- Became one of the leaders of the **Salt Satyagraha in Kerala in 1930**.
- Participated in the **Temple Entry Movement**.
- The movement eventually led to the **Temple Entry Proclamation of 1936**.
- Became the **first President of the Kerala Pradesh Congress Committee (KPCC)**.

### Q43. What did K. Kelappan do after independence?

**Answer:**

- Worked with the **Sarvodaya movement**.
- Worked with the **Bhoodan movement**.
- Promoted:
  - Gandhian principles
  - Rural upliftment

He is remembered as both a **freedom fighter and social reformer**, and his values continue to influence Gandhian institutions and education policies in Kerala.

# 11. T.K. MADHAVAN

### Q45. What was T.K. Madhavan's role in the Vaikom Satyagraha?

- However, Madhavan had to continue the struggle for **temple entry**.

### Q46. What were T.K. Madhavan's other organizational, journalistic and political contributions?

  - Ezhava community

- Helped bring the **Vaikom Satyagraha to national attention** by convincing **Mahatma Gandhi** to support it.
- Led the **Temple Entry Movement**.
- Advocated the right of all castes to enter temples.
- Elected to the **Sree Moolam Praja Sabha in 1918**.
- Used his position to campaign for educational and social rights.

**Current relevance:**

- Pioneer of temple-entry and civil-rights movements in Kerala.
- Commemorated in Kerala's public memory as a **martyr for social justice**.

# 13. KURIAKOSE ELIAS CHAVARA

### Q50. Who was Kuriakose Elias Chavara?

**Answer:**

- Born: **10 February 1805**
- Birthplace: **Kainakary, Alappuzha**
- Died: **3 January 1871**
- Canonized: **23 November 2014**
- Canonized as a Saint by the **Catholic Church**.
- Catholic priest
- Religious leader
- Philosopher
- Social reformer
- Member of the **Syro-Malabar Church**
- First canonized Catholic male saint of Indian origin.

He was:

- Co-founder of the **Carmelites of Mary Immaculate (CMI)**
- First prior general of the first congregation for men in the Syro-Malabar Church.

### Q51. What did Kuriakose Elias Chavara do to expand education among marginalized communities?

**Answer:**

- Established an institution for **Sanskrit studies at Mannanam**.
- A tutor belonging to the **Variar community** was brought from Thrissur to teach there.
- Later established a school at **Arpookara** for the converted Pulaya community.
- He admitted untouchables to schools.
- He also provided them **Sanskrit education**, which had been forbidden to lower castes.
- Through this, he challenged caste-based social restrictions in the early part of the 19th century.
- He pioneered **parish-based schools in Kerala**, commonly known as **Pallikudams/Pallikkoodams**.
- Promoted education for:
  - Girls
  - Lower castes

The source describes the Pallikkoodam system attached to every church as the **first initiative for universal education**.

### Q52. How was Kuriakose Chavara associated with the midday meal system and Pidiyari?

**Answer:**

Chavara believed that the schools at **Mannanam and Arpookara** would succeed only if poor students, particularly Dalits, were provided with **midday meals**.

- The source describes this as his original idea.
- It later inspired **Sir C. P. Ramaswamy Iyer** to recommend the system to the King for implementation in government schools.

Chavara also started the charity practice called **Pidiyari**, meaning **a handful of rice**.

Under this system:

- People set aside a small quantity of rice every day.
- It was stored in a special collection pot.
- The collected rice was taken to church during weekends.
- It was used to feed poor people, especially students through midday meals.

He established the organization:

**“Unnimishihayude Dharma Sabha”**

It managed the Pidiyari collection.

### Q53. What was Chavara's contribution to printing and Malayalam journalism?

**Answer:**

- Started **St. Joseph's Press at Mannanam in 1846**.
- It was the **third printing press in Kerala**.
- It was the **first press founded by a Malayali without foreign assistance**.
- The oldest existing Malayalam newspaper still in circulation, **Nasrani Deepika**, came from this printing press.
- Chavara also established printing presses and published Malayalam literary and devotional works.

### Q54. What are the important work and other innovations associated with Kuriakose Elias Chavara?

**Answer:**

Important work:

- **Atmanutapam** – a spiritual autobiography written in verse.

Other contributions:

- Introduced the **Sunday homily**.
- Introduced other liturgical and social innovations.

**Current relevance:**

- Revered as a pioneer of **modern education and social upliftment in Kerala**.
- His institutions continue to be influential, including:
  - CMI schools
  - Seminaries
  - Printing presses
