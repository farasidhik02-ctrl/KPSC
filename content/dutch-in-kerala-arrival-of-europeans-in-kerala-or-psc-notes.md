### 1. Who were the Dutch and what was their objective in Kerala?

**Answer:**

- The **Dutch** were the **first Protestant nation of Europe** to establish trade contacts with Kerala.
- They challenged the **[Portuguese](https://www.keralapscnotes.qd.je/content/portuguese-in-kerala-arrival-of-europeans-in-kerala-psc-notes/) monopoly over trade**.
- The **Dutch East India Company (VOC)** was established in **1602**.
- Their trade relations with Kerala rulers posed a major challenge to Portuguese commercial monopoly.
- Unlike the Portuguese, the Dutch focused mainly on the **expansion of commerce** and largely kept religious policy aside.
- They maintained good **political and economic relations** with the major rulers of Kerala.

### 2. What was the first political agreement between the Dutch and the Zamorin?

**Answer:**

- In **1604**, Dutch Admiral **Steven Van der Hagen** reached **Calicut**.
- He signed the **first political agreement between the Dutch and the Zamorin**.
- The agreement provided for a **mutual alliance** between the Dutch and the Zamorin.
- Their common objective was to **expel the Portuguese** from the region.
- The treaty also allowed the Dutch to establish **trade facilities at Calicut**.

### 3. How did Dutch power develop in Kerala during the early 17th century?

**Answer:**

- During the early decades of the **17th century**, Portuguese power in Malabar was deteriorating.
- At the same time, Dutch power was expanding.
- The Dutch became a serious threat to **Portuguese stability** in Malabar.
- The Dutch established friendly **political and commercial relations** with rulers of Central Kerala.
- The **Raja of Purakkad** and the **Raja of Kayamkulam** agreed to conduct trade with the Dutch.
- These arrangements helped the Dutch secure a **monopoly over the spice trade by the middle of the 17th century**.

# CONQUEST OF QUILON

### 4. When and how did the Dutch conquer Quilon?

**Answer:**

- After eliminating [Portuguese power](https://www.keralapscnotes.qd.je/content/portuguese-in-kerala-arrival-of-europeans-in-kerala-psc-notes/) from **Ceylon**, the Dutch began their political conquest of **Quilon in 1658**.
- Dutch Admiral **Van Goens** captured the **Portuguese fortress of Quilon**.
- He then signed a treaty with the **Queen of Quilon**.
- The treaty gave the Dutch an **exclusive monopoly over the spice trade**.
- Portuguese possessions were transferred to the **Dutch**.

# CONQUEST OF COCHIN

### 5. Why is the conquest of Cochin important in Dutch history in Kerala?

**Answer:**

- The **real history of the Dutch in Kerala** is considered to have begun with the **conquest of Cochin in 1663**.
- The political situation in Cochin was marked by internal dissension between:
  - **Mutha Thavazhi**
  - **Elaya Thavazhi**
- The Dutch were invited by **Paliath Achan** on behalf of the **Mutha Thavazhi**.
- The Dutch were supported by:
  - **Zamorin**
  - **Raja of Vadakkumkur**
  - **Raja of Tekkumkur**

### 6. How did the Dutch capture Cochin in 1663?

**Answer:**

- In **1663**, the Dutch force under **Van Goens** marched against the **Mattanchery Palace**.
- The battle resulted in severe casualties.
- The Dutch helped reinstall the **prince of the Mutha Thavazhi** as the ruler of Cochin.
- However, the Portuguese challenge still remained.
- Portuguese resistance continued.
- The Dutch captured Portuguese strongholds in **Ernakulam** with the help of the **Anchi Kaimals**.
- With the assistance of **Vira Kerala Varma** and his Prime Minister **Paliath Achan**, the Dutch captured **Cochin Fort on 6 January 1663**.
- The Raja of Cochin accepted **Dutch suzerainty**.
- The treaty effectively made the **Raja of Cochin a puppet in the hands of the Dutch**.

# IMPORTANT DUTCH TREATIES AND BUILDINGS

### 7. What was the Treaty of Azheekod?

**Answer:**

- The **Treaty of Azheekod** was concluded between the **Zamorin and the Dutch in 1661**.

### 8. What are the important places and buildings associated with Dutch rule in Kerala?

**Answer:**

- **Pallipuram** was the place where the Dutch constructed a **hospital for leprosy patients**.
- The **Cochin Bolgatty Palace** was constructed by the Dutch in **1744**.
- The Dutch constructed **Chettuva Fort** near **Guruvayur**.

# BATTLE OF COLACHEL

### 9. What was the Battle of Colachel and when was it fought?

**Answer:**

- The **Battle of Colachel (Kulachal)** was fought between:
  - **Kingdom of Travancore**
  - **Dutch East India Company**
- It took place in **August 1741**.
- The key date given in the notes is **10 August 1741**.
- The battle resulted in a famous victory for **Travancore**.
- It effectively ended Dutch colonial ambitions in the **Indian subcontinent**.
- It is considered one of the earliest instances of an **organised Asian power defeating a European army**.

# EVENTS LEADING TO THE BATTLE OF COLACHEL

### 10. What was the political situation in Kerala before the Battle of Colachel?

**Answer:**

- In the early 18th century, Kerala consisted of several **small kingdoms**.
- These kingdoms were frequently engaged in warfare with one another.
- In the **1730s**, **Marthanda Varma**, the new ruler of Travancore, adopted an **expansionist policy**.
- He conquered several small kingdoms of Malabar.
- These conquests threatened the **economic interests of the Dutch East India Company**.

### 11. How did the Dutch respond to the expansion of Marthanda Varma?

**Answer:**

- **Gustaaf Willem van Imhoff**, the Dutch Governor of **Ceylon (Sri Lanka)**, visited Kochi.
- He recommended military action to protect Dutch trade in Malabar.
- The Dutch formed a military alliance with Travancore's neighbouring powers, including:
  - **Quilon**
  - **Cochi**
  - **Kayamkulam**
- Van Imhoff initially used diplomacy to avoid war.
- He personally met **Marthanda Varma** to negotiate peace.

### 12. What happened during the negotiations between Van Imhoff and Marthanda Varma?

**Answer:**

- During the subsequent meeting, **Van Imhoff threatened war** if Travancore did not accept the Dutch terms.
- Marthanda Varma reportedly laughed off the threat.
- He stated that even if the Dutch defeated him, he would one day invade the **Netherlands and Europe itself with fishing boats**.
- The negotiations eventually broke down.
- War became inevitable.

### 13. How did the Dutch war against Travancore begin?

**Answer:**

- In **late 1739**, the Dutch army in Malabar declared war on Travancore.
- Dutch troops from **Ceylon** were deployed.
- The Dutch and their allies initially achieved several victories.
- They forced the Travancore army at **Kollam** to retreat.
- The **British East India Company**, concerned about the growing power of Travancore, supported the Dutch.
- The British East India Company had been founded on **31 December 1600**.
- The British sent **150 men** to supplement the Dutch forces.
- The combined Dutch army then marched towards **Attingal**.
- They took advantage of the Travancore withdrawal caused by an **invasion by Arcot from the south**.

# SIEGE OF COLACHEL

### 14. How did the Siege of Colachel begin?

**Answer:**

- On **26 November 1740**, the Dutch sent a naval force to **Colachel**.
- The Dutch bombarded the town.
- The bombardment forced the inhabitants to abandon the town.
- Marthanda Varma responded by sending a force of **2,000 soldiers** to Colachel.
- The Dutch planned to occupy Colachel as a step towards capturing **Padmanabhapuram**, the capital of Travancore.
- After capturing Colachel, the Dutch began occupying nearby villages.
- They plundered the region between **Colachel and Kottar**.

### 15. What reinforcements did the Dutch seek during the Siege of Colachel?

**Answer:**

- The Dutch requested reinforcements from:
  - **Sri Lanka**
  - **Indonesia**
- The authorities in Indonesia were occupied with a **native rebellion** and could not provide the expected support.
- However, about **30,000 additional men from Ceylon** arrived as reinforcements, according to the notes.

### 16. How did Marthanda Varma turn the situation against the Dutch?

**Answer:**

- Marthanda Varma took advantage of the shortage of Dutch troops.
- He launched an offensive to **recapture Colachel**.
- He used **patrol boats** to block Dutch supplies coming by sea.
- He laid siege to Colachel.
- The Dutch were surrounded by a **1,000-strong Travancore army**.
- Although the Dutch had technological superiority and were able to inflict casualties on the Travancore forces, they faced serious difficulties.
- **Rains and floods** prevented the Dutch from receiving supplies.
- Much of their **gunpowder became wet**, preventing them from offering effective resistance.

### 17. What happened on 5 August 1741 during the Battle of Colachel?

**Answer:**

- On **5 August 1741**, a cannonball fired by the Travancore army hit a **barrel of gunpowder** inside the Dutch garrison.
- The explosion destroyed the:
  - **Ammunition store**
  - **Food stores**
- The Dutch were left without sufficient ammunition and food.
- They were therefore forced to surrender on **7 August 1741**.
- Some historians consider **10 August 1741** as the official date of surrender.

### 18. Who was the Dutch general who surrendered at Colachel?

**Answer:**

- **D' Lannoy** was the Dutch general who surrendered to **Marthanda Varma**.
- He later became an important military figure in Travancore.
- His burial is associated with **Udayagiri Fort near Thakkala, Tamil Nadu**.

### 19. What was the significance of the Battle of Colachel?

**Answer:**

- The victory established the military strength of **Travancore**.
- It effectively ended Dutch colonial ambitions in the Indian subcontinent.
- It is remembered as one of the earliest major victories of an **Asian power over a European army**.
- The defeat seriously weakened Dutch political ambitions in Kerala.

# END OF DUTCH POLITICAL POWER

### 20. What marked the decline of Dutch political power in Kerala?

**Answer:**

- The **Treaty of Mavelikara** marked the eclipse of the Dutch as a major political power in Kerala.
- The treaty was concluded in **1753**.
- The date given in the notes is **15 August 1753**.
- After this, Dutch political influence in Kerala declined sharply.

# HORTUS MALABARICUS

### 21. What is Hortus Malabaricus?

**Answer:**

- **Hortus Malabaricus** is a **17th-century botanical treatise** dealing with the medicinal properties of the flora of the **Malabar Coast**.
- It was compiled by **Hendrik van Rheede**.
- Van Rheede was the **Governor of Dutch Malabar from 1669 to 1676**.
- It was compiled in the **Latin language**.
- It is also known as **Kerala Ramam**.
- It was published from **Amsterdam**.
- It was published between **1678 and 1703**.
- It consists of **12 volumes**.

### 22. Who was Itty Achudan and what was his role in Hortus Malabaricus?

**Answer:**

- **Itty Achudan** was a distinguished herbalist.
- He belonged to the ancient traditional **Ezhava physician tradition of Kerala**.
- He was the **key informant of Van Rheede**.
- He disclosed traditional **Ayurvedic knowledge about the plants of Malabar** to Van Rheede.
- The physicians and scholars who assisted in the work included:
  - **Itty Achudan**
  - **Ranga Bhat**
  - **Vinayaka Bhat**
  - **Appu Bhat**

### 23. What are the important plant-related facts about Hortus Malabaricus?

**Answer:**

- The **first plant mentioned** in Hortus Malabaricus is the **coconut**.
- The **last plant mentioned** is the **banyan tree**.

### 24. Who else assisted Van Rheede in compiling Hortus Malabaricus?

**Answer:**

- Van Rheede was assisted by the **King of Cochin**.
- He was also assisted by the **Zamorin of Calicut**.
- Important contributors included:
  - **Itty Achudan**
  - **Ranga Bhat**
  - **Vinayaka Bhat**
  - **Appu Bhat**

### 25. When and where were English and Malayalam translations of Hortus Malabaricus published?

**Answer:**

- The **English translation** was published by the **University of Kerala in 2003**.
- The **Malayalam translation** was published by the **University of Kerala in 2008**.
- These translations were largely due to the efforts of **Professor K. S. Manilal**.
