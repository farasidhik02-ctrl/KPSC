## 1. POLITICAL MOVEMENTS IN MALABAR

![](/uploads/Nationalist-movement-in-kerala.webp)

### Emergence of Nationalism

### Q1. When did nationalism emerge in India, and how was Kerala connected with it?

**Answer:**

- The **last decades of the 19th century** saw the emergence of nationalism in India.
- The **Indian National Congress (INC)** was established in **1885**.
- It soon became the spearhead of the **Indian Nationalist Movement**.
- These developments also influenced Kerala.

## 2. MALABAR DISTRICT

### Q2. How did Malabar come under British administration?

**Answer:**

- After the **Anglo-Mysore Wars**, parts of the Malabar Coast that became British colonies were organized into a district of **British India**.
- In **1800**, Malabar was transferred to the **Madras Presidency**.
- Administrative headquarters: **Calicut (Kozhikode)**.
- **Major Macleod** became the Principal Collector on **1 October 1801**.
- The East India Company entered into separate fresh treaties with **Travancore and Kochi**.
- Under the **Treaty of 1791**, the Raja of Kochi agreed to become a **vassal of the English**.

# 3. MALABAR REBELLION — EARLY BACKGROUND

### Q3. What factors led to the early Mappila rebellions in Malabar?

**Answer:**

According to the notes:

- The rebellions were fuelled by:
  - Fiery speeches of Muslim religious leaders
  - Anti-British sentiments
- The **Mappilas** launched violent rebellions.
- Violence was directed against:
  - British authorities
  - Hindu landlords
- Most landlords were **Namboodiri Brahmins**.
- Most tenants were **Mappila Muslims**.

The nature of the rebellion has been interpreted differently:

- Some viewed it as **religious fanaticism**.
- Others regarded it as a **struggle against British authority**.
- Another interpretation sees it as a **peasant revolt against unfair landlord practices**.
- The notes state that a broad consensus sees it as beginning as a struggle against **political power** and later acquiring a **communal colour**.

### Q4. What consequences of the Malabar riots are mentioned in the notes?

**Answer:**

The source states that the riots resulted in:

- Mass killings of **over 10,000 Hindus**
- Rape of women
- Forced religious conversions
- Destruction or damage of nearly **300 temples**
- Loot and arson of property worth crores of rupees
- Burning of Hindu houses

These figures and characterizations are presented here as given in the source notes.

### Q5. What was the T.L. Strange Committee, and what did Strange conclude?

**Answer:**

- **T.L. Strange Committee** was a special committee appointed to investigate the **Moplah disturbances in Malabar**.
- Year: **1852**
- Strange concluded that the outbreaks were caused by **religious fanaticism rather than agrarian discontent**.

### Q6. What happened to H.V. Connolly, and what role did William Logan later play in Malabar?

**Answer:**

**H.V. Connolly:**

- District Magistrate of Malabar.
- Murdered in **1855**.
- Four Mappila convicts killed him at his bungalow in **Calicut**.

**William Logan:**

- Appointed **District Judge of South Malabar in 1875**.
- Studied:
  - Agricultural problems of the Mappila taluks
  - Immigration problems created by colonial rule.

# 4. LOGAN REPORT — 1881

### Q7. What did William Logan conclude about the causes of Mappila revolts?

**Answer:**

The **Logan Report – 1881** gave importance to agrarian causes.

Logan described the Mappila outrages as an attempt to counter the growing power of the **Janmis**, backed by British courts, particularly their new powers of:

- Ouster
- Eviction for rent

In a second investigation in **1880**, Logan concluded:

- Revolts were caused by **Janmi oppression of tenants**.
- Evictions increased enormously between **1860 and 1880**.

He recommended laws to:

- Secure **land tenure**
- Provide **compensation after eviction**.

### Q8. What legislation followed the Logan Report, and why did it fail to solve the peasants' problems?

**Answer:**

The British government introduced land-reform legislation in Malabar.

**Malabar Compensation for Tenants Improvements Act, 1887**

- Required landlords to compensate tenants for improvements they had made to the land.

However:

- The Act was weakly implemented.
- Its successor, the **1900 Act**, also failed to substantially improve peasant conditions.

In **1915**, **Innes, Collector of Malabar**, argued that stronger legislation was necessary to deal with:

- Security of tenure
- Rent-racking
- High renewal fees
- Social tyranny

The British government did not act on these concerns.

# 5. MALABAR MANUAL

### Q9. What is the Malabar Manual, and who wrote it?

**Answer:**

- Written by **William Logan**.
- Official title: **Malabar**
- Popularly known as the **Malabar Manual**.
- Published in **1887**.
- Commissioned by the **Government of Madras**.
- Originally published in **two volumes**.
- Compiled during Logan's tenure as **Collector of Malabar**.
- It served as a guide to the **Malabar District under the Madras Presidency**.

It provides extensive information about:

- Geography
- People
- Religions
- Castes
- Languages
- Culture
- Life and lifestyle of the vernacular people of Malabar
- Life of members of the **East India Company**.

# 6. FIRST WORLD WAR AND THE CALIPHATE

### Q10. How did the First World War affect Muslim political sentiment in India?

**Answer:**

**First World War: 1914–1919**

- Britain and Turkey were on opposing sides.
- Turkey was home to the **Ottoman Empire**.
- It was the seat of the Muslim **Khalifa or Caliphate**.
- The **Khalifa/Caliph** was regarded as:
  - Leader of the Muslim Ummah
  - Political leader
  - Religious leader of Muslims throughout the world
- The Ottoman Empire's ability to resist Europe was a matter of pride.

The notes state:

- Britain attempted to sabotage Turkey's Ottoman Empire.
- The fall of Turkey in **November 1918** caused grief across the Muslim world.
- Indian Muslims became hostile towards the British because Britain was held responsible for Turkey's misfortune.

# 7. KHILAFAT MOVEMENT

### Q11. Why was the Khilafat Movement launched, and who led it?

**Answer:**

During the First World War:

- Turkey was one of the **Central Powers**.
- It fought against Britain.
- After Turkey's defeat, dissolution of the **Ottoman Caliphate** was proposed.
- Muslims regarded the **Sultan of Turkey as their Caliph**, or religious head.

The Khilafat Movement was led by:

- **Maulana Mohammed Ali**
- **Maulana Shaukat Ali**
- Together known as the **Ali Brothers**
- **Maulana Azad**
- **Hakim Ajmal Khan**
- **Hasrat Mohani**

**Mahatma Gandhi** supported the movement in its effort to persuade Britain not to abolish the Caliphate.

The Khilafat leaders accepted Gandhi's **Non-Cooperation Movement**, creating a joint protest against British rule.

### Q12. What was the Khilafat Committee in India?

**Answer:**

Its objective was to:

- Revive and restore the **“glory of Islam”**
- Restore the **Turkish Caliphate**

In India:

- First branch: **Bombay**
- Date: **March 1919**
- Led by the **Ali Brothers**:
  - Maulana Mohammed Ali
  - Maulana Shaukat Ali.

### Q13. Why did the Khilafat Movement decline?

**Answer:**

The notes list the following factors:

- Withdrawal of support by the **Congress**
- British abandonment of the policy of discrimination against Muslims
- Reservation of seats for Muslims in **Central and Provincial Legislatures under the Act of 1919**
- **Mustafa Kamal Pasha** assumed power in Turkey.
- The **Caliphate was abolished**.

# 8. NON-COOPERATION MOVEMENT

### Q14. What happened at the Nagpur Session of the INC in December 1920?

**Answer:**

- The annual INC session was held at **Nagpur, Maharashtra**, in **December 1920**.
- President: **C. Vijayaraghavachariar**
- The INC passed its main resolution on **Non-Cooperation** at this session.

The Gandhi-Ali Brothers partnership contributed to Hindu-Muslim unity through the combined **Khilafat–Non-Cooperation Movement** against British rule.

### Q15. What were the major features of the Non-Cooperation Movement?

**Answer:**

The movement was intended to be a **peaceful and non-violent protest** against British rule.

People were asked to:

- Give up titles.
- Resign from nominated seats in local bodies.
- Resign from government jobs.
- Withdraw children from government-controlled or government-aided schools and colleges.
- Boycott foreign goods.
- Use Indian-made goods.
- Boycott elections to legislative councils.
- Refuse to serve in the British army.

If these measures failed:

- Refusal to pay taxes was also planned.

Other key points:

- INC demanded **Swarajya or self-government**.
- Only completely **non-violent methods** were to be used.
- It marked an important change because the INC was prepared to move beyond constitutional methods to obtain self-rule.
- Gandhi said **Swaraj could be achieved within a year** if the movement was carried through to completion.
- Gandhi considered the **Khilafat issue a golden opportunity for Hindu-Muslim unity** and urged Hindus to support it.

# 9. HOME RULE MOVEMENT AND MALABAR

### Q16. Who started the Home Rule Movement, and what was its wider significance?

**Answer:**

The Home Rule Movement was started by:

- **Annie Besant**
- **Bal Gangadhar Tilak**

Its significance:

- Set the stage for the **Non-Cooperation Movement**.
- United the extremists and moderates of the INC.
- The **Lucknow Pact** brought solidarity between the Muslim League and Congress.
- Return of the extremists gave the INC a more militant character.

### Q17. How did the Home Rule and nationalist movements develop in Malabar during and after the First World War?

**Answer:**

- The **Great War (1914–1918)** gave momentum to the nationalist movement in Malabar.
- In **1916**, a public meeting was held at **Calicut Town Hall** to mobilize support for the government's war efforts.
- **K.P. Kesava Menon** was denied permission by **Collector Innes** to address the meeting in Malayalam.
- After the war came the **Montague-Chelmsford Reforms of 1919**.

**Fifth Malabar District Political Conference:**

- Venue: **Manjeri**
- Date: **28 April 1920**
- **Dr. Annie Besant** was present.
- It passed a resolution describing the reforms as **unsatisfactory and disappointing**.

Malabar also witnessed widespread boycott of:

- Foreign goods
- Courts of law
- Educational institutions

Meanwhile:

- Khilafat agitation developed against British policy.
- For some time, the **Congress and Khilafat movements merged into one movement**.
- Hindus and Muslims participated enthusiastically.

# 10. MALABAR REBELLION OF 1921

### Q18. How did the Khilafat and nationalist movements contribute to developments in Malabar before the 1921 rebellion?

**Answer:**

- **Mahatma Gandhi** and **Maulana Shaukat Ali** visited Malabar.
- They organized **Khilafat Committees**.
- They also sought to accelerate the nationalist struggle against foreign rule.
- **1921** saw the most important episode of the Malabar Rebellion.
- Most affected areas:
  - **Ernad**
  - **Valluvanad**
- Both taluks were placed under **Section 144**.

### Q19. What was the Pukottur Riot?

**Answer:**

- Place: **Pukottur, Ernad taluk**
- Date given: **26 August 1921**
- Authorities attempted to arrest **Vadakke Veettil Muhammad**.
- He was secretary of the local **Khilafat Committee at Pukottur**.
- The attempted arrest led to violent clashes between:
  - Police
  - Mappilas

During the rebellion, Mappilas:

- Attacked police stations
- Looted government treasuries
- Destroyed government offices.

### Q20. Who were the major leaders and important centres associated with this phase of the movement?

**Answer:**

Major leaders listed in the notes:

- **Variankunnath Kunjahammad Haji**
- **Sithi Koya Tangal of Kumaranputhur**
- **Ali Musaliyar**

The notes list these as major centres of the Home Rule Movement in Kerala:

- Malappuram
- Tirurangadi
- Manjeri
- Perinthalmanna

The notes also state:

- **Ali Musaliyar ousted the British Government and formed a new government.**

### Q21. What was the Wagon Tragedy?

**Answer:**

- Date: **20 November 1921**
- **90 Mappila prisoners** were transported in a closed railway goods wagon.
- Route: **Tirur → Coimbatore**
- By the time the train reached **Podanur**, **61 of the 90 prisoners had died of suffocation**.

Historian **Sumit Sarkar** described the incident as:

**“Blackhole of Podannur.”**

He drew a parallel with the alleged **Black Hole tragedy of 1756**, which the notes state most recent studies say did not occur as traditionally described in history books.

# 13. LAHORE SESSION AND PURNA SWARAJ

### Q25. What happened at the Lahore Session of the INC in 1929?

**Answer:**

- Date: **19 December 1929**
- Venue: **Lahore**
- President: **Jawaharlal Nehru**
- INC passed the historic **Purna Swaraj or Total Independence Resolution**.

A public declaration was made on:

**26 January 1930**

- Congress urged Indians to observe the day as **Independence Day**.

The resolution followed the breakdown of negotiations between:

- Indian freedom movement leaders
- British government

The dispute concerned **Dominion Status for India**.

# 15. CONGRESS SOCIALIST PARTY — 1934

### Q29. What was the Congress Socialist Party, and what ideology did it follow?

**Answer:**

- **Congress Socialist Party (CSP)** was founded in **1934**.
- It functioned as a **socialist caucus within the Indian National Congress**.

According to the notes, its founders rejected:

- What they considered Gandhi's **anti-rational mysticism**
- The Communist Party of India's **sectarian attitude towards Congress**

It was influenced by:

- **Fabianism**
- **Marxism-Leninism**

It included supporters of armed struggle or sabotage such as:

- **Yusuf Meherally**
- **Jai Prakash Narayan**
- **Basawon Singh (Sinha)**

It also included supporters of **Ahimsa/non-violent resistance**, such as:

- **Acharya Narendra Deva**.

### Q30. What economic system did the CSP advocate, and who were its important Kerala leaders?

**Answer:**

CSP advocated **decentralized socialism**.

A substantial share of economic power was to be held by:

- Cooperatives
- Trade unions
- Independent farmers
- Local authorities

Important CSP leaders in Kerala:

- **E.M.S.**
- **P. Krishna Pillai**.

# 17. AMARAVATHI SATYAGRAHA — 1961

### Q33. What was the Amaravathi Satyagraha, and why did A.K. Gopalan lead it?

**Answer:**

**Amaravathi** is a village near **Kumily** in the High Ranges, associated with cultivation of:

- Coffee
- Pepper
- Cardamom
- Tea

On **6 June 1961**:

- Communist veteran **A.K. Gopalan (AKG)** began a satyagraha at Amaravathi.
- Its purpose was to protect around **1,600 people** who had been displaced.
- Their land had been acquired for the **Idukki Hydroelectric Project at Ayyappancoil**.
- They had been removed from their land and left at Amaravathi.
