## Glorious Revolution / Bloodless Revolution — 1688

![](/uploads/Great-Revolution-in-England.webp)

### Q1. What was the Great Revolution in England, and by what other names is it known?

**Answer:**

- The **Great Revolution in England** generally refers to the **Glorious Revolution of 1688–1689**.
- It resulted in:
  - The overthrow and exile of **King James II**
  - The accession of **William III (William of Orange)** and **Mary II**
  - A major strengthening of **Parliament's authority over the monarchy**
- It is commonly known as:
  - **Glorious Revolution**
  - **Bloodless Revolution**
  - **Revolution of 1688**

It is called “Bloodless” because the transfer of power in **England** involved relatively little bloodshed. However, the wider revolution was **not bloodless in Ireland and Scotland**.

# 1. BACKGROUND — THE STUART MONARCHY

### Q2. What was the basic political conflict behind the Great Revolution?

**Answer:**

The fundamental conflict was over the relationship between the **monarchy and Parliament**.

Important issues included:

- Extent of the King's powers
- Authority of Parliament
- Taxation and laws
- Religious policy
- Fear of arbitrary or absolute monarchy
- Conflict between **Catholicism and Protestantism**

The Revolution eventually established more firmly that the monarch could not govern independently of Parliament.

### Q3. Which rulers immediately preceded the Glorious Revolution?

**Answer:**

Remember the sequence:

**Charles I → Commonwealth/Protectorate → Charles II → James II → William III & Mary II**

Important points:

- **Charles I** was executed in **1649** following the English Civil War.
- Monarchy was restored in **1660** under **Charles II**.
- This is known as the **Restoration**.
- Charles II died in **1685**.
- His brother **James II** became King in **1685**.
- James II's reign ultimately produced the crisis that led to the Glorious Revolution.

# 2. JAMES II

### Q4. Who was James II, and why did his rule become unpopular?

**Answer:**

**James II:**

- Became King in **1685**.
- Was a committed **Roman Catholic**.
- Ruled a country in which Protestantism was dominant.

Opposition developed because of fears concerning:

- His Catholic religious policies
- His use of **royal prerogative**
- His attempts to suspend or dispense with laws
- His attempts to give Catholics greater access to public offices
- His maintenance of a **standing army**
- His attempts to exercise authority without sufficient parliamentary control

His policies created fears of both **Catholic domination** and **arbitrary monarchy**.

# 3. RELIGIOUS ISSUE

### Q5. Why was religion an important cause of the Glorious Revolution?

**Answer:**

England was predominantly **Protestant**, while James II was a **Catholic monarch**.

James:

- Gave Catholics greater religious freedom.
- Allowed Catholics to hold important positions.
- Used royal powers to bypass restrictions imposed by existing laws.
- Tried to promote greater religious toleration through the **Declaration of Indulgence**.

Many opponents believed his policies threatened:

- The **Church of England**
- Protestant succession
- The authority of Parliament.

### Q6. What was the Declaration of Indulgence, and how did it contribute to the crisis?

**Answer:**

James II issued a **Declaration of Indulgence**.

Its purpose included relaxing religious restrictions on:

- Catholics
- Protestant Nonconformists/Dissenters

However, an important issue was **how James exercised this power**.

- He claimed the royal power to suspend laws.
- This increased fears that the King could override laws without Parliament.
- In **1688**, seven bishops opposed his order concerning the Declaration.
- James prosecuted the **Seven Bishops**.
- They were **acquitted in June 1688**.

Their acquittal was a major political setback for James II.

# 4. BIRTH OF JAMES II'S SON

### Q7. Why was the birth of James II's son in 1688 a turning point?

**Answer:**

Before 1688:

- James II's Protestant daughter **Mary** was expected to succeed him.
- Mary was married to the Protestant **William of Orange**.

But on **10 June 1688**:

- James II and his Catholic wife **Mary of Modena** had a son.
- His name was **James Francis Edward Stuart**.

This created the possibility of a continuing **Catholic royal succession**.

The birth greatly increased Protestant fears and helped trigger the political crisis that led to the Revolution.

# 5. WILLIAM OF ORANGE AND THE “IMMORTAL SEVEN”

### Q8. Who was William of Orange, and what was his relationship with James II?

**Answer:**

**William of Orange:**

- Protestant ruler from the **Dutch Republic**.
- Also known as **William, Prince of Orange**.
- He was both the **nephew and son-in-law of James II**.
- He was married to **Mary**, James II's Protestant daughter.

He later became:

**King William III of England.**

### Q9. Who were the “Immortal Seven,” and what did they do?

**Answer:**

The **Immortal Seven** were seven prominent Englishmen who opposed James II.

In **June 1688**, they invited **William of Orange** to intervene in England.

Their concerns included:

- Preservation of Protestantism
- James II's policies
- The new Catholic succession
- Need for a free Parliament

Their invitation helped set William's invasion in motion.

# 6. GLORIOUS REVOLUTION — 1688

### Q10. What happened during the Glorious Revolution of 1688?

**Answer:**

The main sequence was:

**1. William invited**

- The Immortal Seven invited William of Orange to intervene.

**2. William landed in England**

- Date: **5 November 1688**
- He landed with an army in southwest England.

**3. Support for James II collapsed**

- Important military officers and political supporters deserted James.
- Even members of his family moved towards William's side.

**4. James II fled**

- James attempted to escape.
- He eventually fled successfully to **France in December 1688**.

**5. William entered London**

- William reached London on **18 December 1688**.

A **Convention Parliament** was then summoned to determine the political settlement.

# 7. WILLIAM III AND MARY II

### Q11. How did William and Mary become rulers of England?

**Answer:**

After James II fled:

- A **Convention Parliament** met in **January 1689**.
- Parliament worked out a new political settlement.
- The **Declaration of Rights** was presented to William and Mary.
- They accepted the throne on **13 February 1689**.
- William and Mary became **joint monarchs**.
- They were crowned in **April 1689**.

Thus:

**James II → removed/exiled**

**William III + Mary II → joint sovereigns**.

# 8. BILL OF RIGHTS — 1689

### Q12. What was the Bill of Rights of 1689, and why was it important?

**Answer:**

The **Bill of Rights, 1689** was one of the most important results of the Glorious Revolution.

It developed from the **Declaration of Rights** presented to William and Mary.

Its purpose was to:

- Limit arbitrary royal power
- Protect the powers of Parliament
- Establish important rights and liberties
- Regulate the relationship between the **Crown and Parliament**

It became a landmark in the development of **constitutional monarchy and parliamentary government in England**.

### Q13. What were the important provisions of the Bill of Rights?

**Answer:**

Important principles included:

- The monarch could not **suspend laws without Parliament**.
- The monarch could not levy money/taxes for the Crown without parliamentary authorization.
- A **standing army in peacetime** required Parliament's consent.
- Subjects had the **right to petition the King**.
- Elections to Parliament should be **free**.
- **Freedom of speech and debate in Parliament** was protected.
- Excessive bail and excessive fines should not be imposed.
- **Cruel and unusual punishments** were prohibited.
- Parliament should be held **frequently**.

These restrictions significantly strengthened Parliament against arbitrary royal authority.

# 9. TOLERATION ACT — 1689

### Q14. What was the Toleration Act of 1689?

**Answer:**

The **Toleration Act** was passed by Parliament in **1689**, during the reign of William and Mary.

It:

- Gave most **Protestant Dissenters/Nonconformists** freedom to worship publicly under specified conditions.
- Required them to take a simplified oath of allegiance.
- Did **not establish complete religious equality**.
- Dissenters continued to face other restrictions.

It marked an important stage in the gradual extension of religious toleration and civil rights beyond the established Anglican Church.

# 10. CONSTITUTIONAL MONARCHY

### Q16. How did the Glorious Revolution change the nature of monarchy in England?

**Answer:**

The Revolution was a major step towards **constitutional monarchy**.

After the Revolution:

- Monarchs could not govern independently of Parliament in the manner attempted by James II.
- Parliament acquired a stronger position in:
  - Law-making
  - Taxation
  - Control of government finances
  - Military matters
  - Royal succession
- The Crown was increasingly governed within a framework of **law and parliamentary consent**.

The Revolution is therefore considered a major turning point in the development of the **British constitutional system and parliamentary sovereignty**.

# 11. ACT OF SETTLEMENT

### Q17. What was the Act of Settlement, and how was it connected to the Glorious Revolution?

**Answer:**

The constitutional settlement continued after 1689.

The **Act of Settlement 1701**:

- Further regulated succession to the English throne.
- Helped secure a **Protestant succession**.
- Provided that succession would pass through the Protestant descendants of **Sophia, Electress of Hanover**, under its provisions.

It strengthened Parliament's role in determining the **succession to the Crown**.

# 12. MAJOR RESULTS

### Q18. What were the major results and significance of the Great Revolution?

**Answer:**

The important results were:

- **James II was removed** from power.
- **William III and Mary II** became joint monarchs.
- Arbitrary royal authority was restricted.
- **Parliament became more powerful** in relation to the Crown.
- **Bill of Rights, 1689** established important constitutional principles.
- **Toleration Act, 1689** granted limited religious toleration to many Protestant dissenters.
- Protestant succession was strengthened.
- England moved further towards a **constitutional monarchy**.
- Parliament's role in taxation, legislation, the military and succession was strengthened.
- It became a major landmark in the historical development of **parliamentary government**.

# 13. WHY “BLOODLESS REVOLUTION”?

### Q19. Why is the Glorious Revolution called the “Bloodless Revolution”?

**Answer:**

It is traditionally called the **Bloodless Revolution** because the change of regime in **England** in 1688 involved relatively little fighting.

The wider struggle involved serious conflict in:

- **Ireland**
- **Scotland**

Therefore, “Bloodless Revolution” is a conventional name mainly referring to the English transfer of power.

# 14. IMPORTANT PEOPLE

### Q20. Who are the personalities that should be connected with the Great Revolution?

**Answer:**

**James II**

- Catholic King whose policies produced the immediate crisis.
- Fled to France in 1688.

**William of Orange**

- Protestant Dutch ruler.
- Husband and nephew of James II's daughter Mary.
- Invited to England.
- Became **William III**.

**Mary II**

- Protestant daughter of James II.
- Wife of William of Orange.
- Became joint monarch with William.

**Mary of Modena**

- Catholic wife of James II.
- Gave birth to **James Francis Edward Stuart in 1688**.

**Immortal Seven**

- Invited William of Orange to intervene in England.
