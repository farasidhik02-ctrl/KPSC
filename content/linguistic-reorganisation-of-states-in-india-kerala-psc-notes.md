### Background

**Q: What is meant by the linguistic reorganisation of states?**  
A: Reorganisation of state boundaries primarily on the basis of the language spoken by the majority of the population.

**Q: Which organisation first strongly promoted the idea of linguistic provinces during the freedom struggle?**  
A: Indian National Congress.

**Q: At which session did the Indian National Congress reorganise its Provincial Congress Committees on a linguistic basis?**  
A: Nagpur Session, 1920.

**Q: Who was a major early advocate of linguistic provinces in India?**  
A: Mahatma Gandhi.

**Q: Why did the national leadership become cautious about linguistic states after Independence?**  
A: It feared that linguistic divisions could threaten national unity and administrative stability immediately after Partition.

---

# Linguistic Provinces Commission — Dar Commission

**Q: When was the Linguistic Provinces Commission appointed?**  
A: 1948.

**Q: What was another name for the Linguistic Provinces Commission of 1948?**  
A: Dar Commission.

**Q: Who chaired the Linguistic Provinces Commission?**  
A: S. K. Dar.

**Q: What was the purpose of the Dar Commission?**  
A: To examine the feasibility of reorganising provinces on a linguistic basis.

**Q: What did the Dar Commission recommend regarding linguistic states?**  
A: It opposed reorganising states solely on the basis of language.

**Q: What factors did the Dar Commission consider more important than language?**  
A: Administrative convenience, financial viability and national interest.

**Q: When did the Dar Commission submit its report?**  
A: December 1948.

---

# JVP Committee

**Q: Which committee was formed after the Dar Commission to examine the issue of linguistic provinces?**  
A: JVP Committee.

**Q: When was the JVP Committee appointed?**  
A: 1948.

**Q: What does JVP stand for?**  
A: Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel and Pattabhi Sitaramayya.

**Q: Who were the members of the JVP Committee?**  
A: Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel and Pattabhi Sitaramayya.

**Q: When did the JVP Committee submit its report?**  
A: April 1949.

**Q: What was the JVP Committee's position on linguistic reorganisation?**  
A: It rejected the immediate reorganisation of states solely on linguistic grounds.

**Q: What did the JVP Committee consider more important than linguistic demands?**  
A: National unity, security and economic development.

**Q: Did the JVP Committee completely rule out linguistic states forever?**  
A: No. It held that linguistic reorganisation could be considered later if there was strong public demand and other conditions were favourable.

# Andhra Movement and Potti Sriramulu

**Q: Which movement became the immediate catalyst for linguistic reorganisation in India?**  
A: Movement for a separate Telugu-speaking Andhra State.

**Q: From which state was Andhra State carved out?**  
A: Madras State.

**Q: Who undertook a fast unto death demanding a separate Andhra State?**  
A: Potti Sriramulu.

**Q: What was Potti Sriramulu demanding?**  
A: A separate state for Telugu-speaking people.

**Q: How long did Potti Sriramulu's fast last before his death?**  
A: 58 days.

**Q: When did Potti Sriramulu die?**  
A: 15 December 1952.

**Q: What happened after the death of Potti Sriramulu?**  
A: Widespread protests forced the Union government to accept the demand for a separate Andhra State.

**Q: When was Andhra State formed?**  
A: 1 October 1953.

**Q: What was the significance of Andhra State?**  
A: It was the first state created on a linguistic basis in independent India.

**Q: What was the capital of Andhra State when it was created in 1953?**  
A: Kurnool.

**Q: Who became the first Chief Minister of Andhra State?**  
A: Tanguturi Prakasam (T. Prakasam).

**Q: Which city remained with Madras State despite demands to include it in Andhra?**  
A: Madras (Chennai).

**Q: What slogan became associated with the Telugu claim over Madras city?**  
A: "Madras Manade" (Madras is ours).

**Q: Was Tamil Nadu created as a new state in 1953 along with Andhra?**  
A: No. Andhra State was separated from the existing Madras State in 1953; Madras State was renamed Tamil Nadu only in 1969.

# States Reorganisation Commission (SRC)

**Q: When was the States Reorganisation Commission appointed?**  
A: December 1953.

**Q: Why was the States Reorganisation Commission appointed?**  
A: To comprehensively examine the reorganisation of India's states after growing demands for linguistic states.

**Q: Who was the chairman of the States Reorganisation Commission?**  
A: Fazl Ali.

**Q: Who were the three members of the States Reorganisation Commission?**  
A: Fazl Ali, K. M. Panikkar and H. N. Kunzru.

**Q: Which Malayali historian and diplomat was a member of the States Reorganisation Commission?**  
A: K. M. Panikkar.

**Q: When did the States Reorganisation Commission submit its report?**  
A: 1955.

**Q: Did the SRC accept language as the sole criterion for state reorganisation?**  
A: No.

**Q: What factors did the SRC consider while recommending state boundaries?**  
A: Linguistic and cultural homogeneity, national unity and security, financial and administrative viability, and economic development.

**Q: What was the SRC's general approach towards linguistic states?**  
A: It accepted language as an important basis for reorganisation but subordinated it to national unity and administrative and economic considerations.

**Q: How many states and centrally administered territories did the SRC originally recommend?**  
A: 16 states and 3 centrally administered territories.

**Q: Were all recommendations of the SRC accepted by the Government of India?**  
A: No.

# States Reorganisation Act, 1956

**Q: Which major law implemented the large-scale reorganisation of Indian states?**  
A: States Reorganisation Act, 1956.

**Q: Which constitutional amendment accompanied the States Reorganisation Act, 1956?**  
A: Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956.

**Q: When did the States Reorganisation Act, 1956 come into effect?**  
A: 1 November 1956.

**Q: How many states and Union Territories existed after the 1956 reorganisation?**  
A: 14 states and 6 Union Territories.

**Q: What happened to the old classification of Part A, Part B, Part C and Part D states in 1956?**  
A: It was abolished.

**Q: Which constitutional amendment abolished the distinction between Part A, B, C and D states?**  
A: Seventh Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956.

### 14 States after the 1956 Reorganisation

**Q: Name the 14 states created/recognised following the States Reorganisation Act, 1956.**  
A: Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Bombay, Jammu and Kashmir, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Madras, Mysore, Orissa, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal.

### 6 Union Territories

**Q: Name the six Union Territories after the 1956 reorganisation.**  
A: Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindivi Islands, Manipur and Tripura.

**Q: What is the present name of the Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindivi Islands?**  
A: Lakshadweep.

# Formation of Andhra Pradesh

**Q: When was Andhra Pradesh formed?**  
A: 1 November 1956.

**Q: How was Andhra Pradesh formed in 1956?**  
A: By merging Andhra State with the Telugu-speaking Telangana region of Hyderabad State.

**Q: What was the capital of the newly formed Andhra Pradesh in 1956?**  
A: Hyderabad.

**Q: What was the agreement associated with the merger of Telangana and Andhra in 1956?**  
A: Gentlemen's Agreement.

**Q: What was significant about Andhra Pradesh after its formation in 1956?**  
A: It became the first major state formed by combining Telugu-speaking regions under the States Reorganisation Act.

# Formation of Kerala

**Q: When was Kerala formed?**  
A: 1 November 1956.

**Q: Kerala was formed primarily on what basis?**  
A: Malayalam language.

**Q: Which regions were primarily combined to form Kerala?**  
A: Travancore-Cochin and the Malabar district of Madras State.

**Q: Which taluk of South Kanara district was added to Kerala in 1956?**  
A: Kasaragod taluk.

**Q: Which Malayalam-speaking region of Madras State became part of Kerala?**  
A: Malabar district.

**Q: Which regions of Travancore-Cochin were transferred to Madras State in 1956?**  
A: Agastheeswaram, Thovala, Kalkulam and Vilavancode taluks, along with part of Shencottah (Shenkottai) taluk.

**Q: The four southern taluks transferred from Travancore-Cochin became part of which district?**  
A: Kanyakumari district.

**Q: Why were the Kanyakumari areas transferred from Travancore-Cochin to Madras State?**  
A: They had a predominantly Tamil-speaking population.

**Q: Which movement demanded the merger of Tamil-speaking areas of Travancore-Cochin with Madras State?**  
A: Travancore Tamil Nadu Congress movement.

**Q: Who was a prominent leader of the Travancore Tamil Nadu Congress?**  
A: A. Nesamony.

**Q: What is celebrated in Kerala on 1 November?**  
A: Kerala Piravi.

# Other Major Changes in 1956

**Q: Which state was substantially enlarged by bringing together Kannada-speaking areas in 1956?**  
A: Mysore State.

**Q: When was Mysore State renamed Karnataka?**  
A: 1973.

**Q: Which bilingual state containing Marathi- and Gujarati-speaking regions was created in 1956?**  
A: Bombay State.

**Q: Which former princely state was divided among neighbouring states in the 1956 reorganisation?**  
A: Hyderabad State.

**Q: What happened to Hyderabad State during the 1956 reorganisation?**  
A: Its Telugu-speaking Telangana region joined Andhra Pradesh, while Marathi-speaking and Kannada-speaking areas were merged with Bombay and Mysore respectively.

# Punjab Reorganisation — 1966

**Q: Which linguistic movement led to the reorganisation of Punjab?**  
A: Punjabi Suba Movement.

**Q: What did the Punjabi Suba Movement demand?**  
A: A Punjabi-speaking state.

**Q: Which political organisation was closely associated with the Punjabi Suba demand?**  
A: Shiromani Akali Dal.

**Q: Which Act reorganised Punjab in 1966?**  
A: Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966.

**Q: When did the reorganisation of Punjab take effect?**  
A: 1 November 1966.

**Q: Which new state was created from Punjab in 1966?**  
A: Haryana.

**Q: What was the principal linguistic basis for separating Haryana from Punjab?**  
A: Haryana was predominantly Hindi-speaking, while reorganised Punjab was predominantly Punjabi-speaking.

**Q: What happened to Chandigarh after the Punjab reorganisation?**  
A: It became a Union Territory and the joint capital of Punjab and Haryana.

**Q: Which areas of Punjab were transferred to Himachal Pradesh in 1966?**  
A: Certain hill areas.

# High-Yield Chronology

**Q: Arrange the major linguistic reorganisation developments chronologically.**  
A: Dar Commission (1948) → JVP Committee (1948–49) → Potti Sriramulu's death (1952) → Andhra State (1953) → SRC appointed (1953) → SRC Report (1955) → States Reorganisation Act (1956).

**Q: Arrange these states by year of formation: Andhra State, Maharashtra, Haryana, Kerala.**  
A: Andhra State (1953) → Kerala (1956) → Maharashtra (1960) → Haryana (1966).

**Q: Arrange these later states chronologically: Nagaland, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Goa.**  
A: Nagaland (1963) → Himachal Pradesh (1971) → Sikkim (1975) → Goa (1987).

**Q: Arrange the states created in 2000 chronologically.**  
A: Chhattisgarh (1 November) → Uttarakhand (9 November) → Jharkhand (15 November).

**Q: What are the most important dates in the history of linguistic reorganisation?**  
A: 1948 – Dar Commission; 1949 – JVP report; 1953 – Andhra State and SRC; 1956 – major states reorganisation; 1960 – Maharashtra and Gujarat; 1966 – Punjab and Haryana; 1972 – major Northeast reorganisation; 1987 – Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa; 2000 – Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand; 2014 – Telangana; 2019 – Jammu & Kashmir reorganisation.
